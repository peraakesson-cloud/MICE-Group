# 11 - High-Consideration Travel Conversion, CRM and Email Automation

Research agent: conversion / CRM. Date: 2026-11-02. Client: Swedish long-haul specialist; goal is sydafrikaresor.se from 40 to 80 MSEK in 24 months.
No client data was available. Everything not sourced to a URL is marked [ASSUMPTION].

## 0. Source quality warning

Almost all public "travel conversion benchmarks" come from vendor blogs (CRM, email and agency marketing content), not audited studies. They are directional only. Several cite each other, and the figures are mostly US/UK, not Swedish long-haul. Treat every number below as a hypothesis to be replaced by the client's own CRM data within 60 days. Source tiers: (A) peer-reviewed or large primary dataset, (B) vendor dataset with stated sample, (C) vendor opinion/aggregator with no method.

## 2. Consideration cycle (how long and how many touches)

- Expedia Group / Luth Research (seven countries, not Sweden): average trip consideration window 71 days; 303 minutes of travel content consumed in the 45 days before booking; ~2.5 page views/day early, 25 on the day of purchase. https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/travelers-booking-behavior-path-to-purchase-marketing-strategies and https://www.expedia.com/newsroom/eg-path-to-purchase-research/ (Tier B, online bookings, mostly accommodation/flight).
- Implication: a South Africa leisure buyer is likely in market for months; the CRM must keep a lead warm for 90-180 days. [ASSUMPTION: long-haul custom trips are longer than the 71-day average; verify with the client's inquiry-to-booking lag.]

## 3. Speed to lead

- Harvard Business Review 2011, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (2,241 companies audited, 1.25M leads analysed): firms contacting within one hour were nearly 7x as likely to qualify the lead as firms waiting one more hour, and 60x+ as likely as those waiting 24h+; 37% responded within an hour, 24% took more than 24h, 23% never responded; average 42 hours. https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads and summary with corrections at https://resources.rework.com/libraries/lead-management/lead-response-time (Tier A/B, but B2B-heavy and over 10 years old).
- Widely quoted "5 minutes = 100x contact" comes from the Oldroyd/InsideSales study (~2007, 15,000 leads); treat as directional. https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/speed-to-lead-response-time-benchmarks-2026-data-playbook (Tier C). That page also cites an Optifai benchmark of 32% close under 5 min vs 12% at 24h+, which I could not verify.
- Implication: set an SLA of first human reply under 1 business hour, auto-acknowledgement instantly, same-day proposal outline. Cheap to implement and probably the fastest conversion win.

## 4. Consultative selling and quote management

From the Rework guides (Tier C, listed at https://resources.rework.com/libraries/travel-tour-growth/booking-conversion-metrics): track conversion by agent (best agents often 2-3x weaker ones), by source and by destination; collect enough qualifying detail up front to quote relevantly; offer deposit plus balance and several payment methods; use live chat (claimed +15-30%), social proof (+15-30% claimed), and fewer form fields (each field costs 5-10% claimed). These claimed uplifts are unverified vendor figures; run A/B tests rather than assuming them.
Practical consultative model for 2x volume: qualification call (budget, travellers, month, interests), itinerary sent within 24-48h, one revision loop, scheduled decision call, structured follow-up per section 1. Capacity maths is in section 8.

Caveat: ecommerce flow numbers do not transfer to SEK 30-60k consultative trips. The right transferable lessons are structural: triggered flows beat batch campaigns; the quote and the post-trip moment are the triggers; measure revenue per recipient not opens.

Sweden legal note: the Swedish Marketing Act is reported to require prior consent for marketing email to natural persons, with an exception for existing customers and similar products if an opt-out is offered; B2B to companies is looser. Sources are vendor summaries, so treat as a flag: https://getrebelos.com/blog/b2b-email-rules-sweden , https://revexa.io/en/blog/is-cold-email-legal-in-sweden . Needs verification by the client's legal adviser (also GDPR basis, consent logging, and the Swedish "NIX" style opt-outs for phone). Consequence: build consent capture into every form and quote, and keep a separate customer-based flow.

## 6. Repeat and referral

- Promodo (FREETOUR.com case, a tours marketplace, not Sweden or long-haul): average rebooking interval 312 days; highly engaged repeat users return about every 146 days; reactivation campaign after 3 months inactivity reactivated 400K+ users; warmed-up promotional sends held >50% open vs 15.7% benchmark. https://www.promodo.com/blog/tourism-marketing-benchmarks (Tier C, single vendor case).
- Loyalty programme claims (members book at "15x" repeat rate, spend 79% more, 73% more likely to recommend): Arrivia 2025 as quoted at https://atlasperk.com/guides/crm-automation-for-travel/retention-loyalty/ . Self-selection bias is likely; do not use as a forecast.
- Referral automation recommendation: ask 10-14 days after return, gated by a 4+ star survey, dual reward (same atlasperk page, Tier C).
- Referral leads convert far better (45-60% quote-to-booking per Rework; 9.5% lead-level per ivyforms).
- Missing: I found no credible published repeat rate for specialist long-haul in the Nordics. For South Africa, a repeat purchase of the same destination within 2 years is structurally low; repeat is more plausibly cross-sell (Southern Africa neighbours, other long-haul) and referral. ## 7. Implications for doubling revenue

Revenue = qualified leads x quote rate x close rate x average booking value x (1 + repeat/referral). Doubling revenue does not require doubling every term.

Illustrative model [ASSUMPTION: all inputs; not client data]:
- Today: avg booking value 45,000 SEK per booking group, giving ~889 bookings for 40 MSEK. Needed: ~1,778 bookings for 80 MSEK at constant value.
- With 80% quote rate and 25% close: today ~4,450 inquiries; target at the same ratios ~8,900 inquiries.
- Lever stack (no single lever has to carry the plan):
  1. Close rate 25% to 30% (SLA, structured follow-up, better proposals): +20% revenue.
  2. Inquiry to quote 75% to 85%: +13%.
  3. Average booking value +10% (upsell extensions, better rooms, add-on safaris): +10%.
  4. Repeat/referral share from ~15% to ~30% of sales: roughly +15-18% on the base.
  5. Remaining ~+30-40% must come from more qualified inquiries (paid, SEO, partnerships), which is the work of other research streams.
  Compounded 1.20 x 1.13 x 1.10 x 1.16 = ~1.73 (rounded), so conversion and retention alone plausibly give ~70% of the goal and the rest comes from traffic. This is arithmetic on assumptions, not a forecast.
- Capacity: if each travel consultant can handle X active quotes, doubling quotes needs either more consultants or automation. With 22 staff in total, the sales team size is unknown; see unknowns. Rework suggests agent-level conversion varies 2-3x, so coaching and routing of top leads to top consultants is cheaper than hiring.

CRM requirements to enable the above (Rework tracking guidance plus general practice):
1. Single lead record with source/UTM, consent flag, trip details, quote value, quote age, lost reason.
2. Stage definitions: inquiry, qualified, quote sent, decision call booked, booked, deposit paid, balance paid, travelled, review/referral asked.
3. Measure conversion at 30/60/90 days (late converts) and net of cancellations.
4. Automations: instant acknowledgement; consultant task within 1h; quote follow-up D2/D7/D14/D21/D30; abandoned-inquiry-form recovery; pre-trip sequence; post-trip review plus referral ask at 10-14 days; 3-month and 10-month reactivation (anniversary-based); newsletter segmented by interest (safari, Cape Town and Garden Route, wine, family, self-drive).
5. Weekly dashboard: response time, quote rate, close rate by consultant and by source, pipeline by travel month, revenue per recipient by flow.
