# 05 - South Africa Product and Supply Landscape

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: what Sydafrikaresor.se can sell and source in South Africa, where supply is tight, how to contract it, and what that means for doubling revenue from 40 MSEK to 80 MSEK by end of 2028.

Convention: facts carry a source URL. Anything without a source is tagged [ASSUMPTION] with a rationale and is collected in section 9 so the CEO can replace it with internal data. No internal company data was available; nothing below states the company's own figures except what its public website says (cited).

---

## 1. Executive findings

1. Destination demand is strong and rising. South Africa received 10.5 million international tourists in 2025 (+17.7% vs 2024, +2.6% vs 2019). Overseas arrivals are 22.8% of the total and grew 11.9% in 2025. Source: Stats SA, https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19362
2. Supply, not demand, is the likely binding constraint on a 2x plan. The scarce items are (a) peak-season beds in Sabi Sand and other Greater Kruger private lodges, (b) peak-season Cape Town luxury and upper-scale rooms, (c) Garden Route gateway capacity (George Airport), and (d) long-haul seats from Sweden when Gulf carriers are disrupted.
3. Prices on the supply side are climbing faster than general inflation. Cape Town luxury ADR rose from about USD 125 (2015) to USD 288 (2025), 8.7% CAGR, even with a weakening rand. Source: HVS, https://www.hvs.com/article/10464-cape-town-tourism-shows-resilience-despite-global-uncertainty. Kruger international conservation fee rises from R602 to R692 per adult per day from 1 Nov 2026 (+14.9%, calculated). Source: https://www.getsafaritours.com/tanzania/guides/african-safari-cost and https://krugercompanion.co.za/fees/
4. Revenue can double through price/mix without doubling pax, but only if supply is contracted ahead of demand. Early allocation contracts (10-12 months before peak) are the most important supply action.
5. Safari alternatives that relieve peak pressure exist and are under-marketed to Swedes: malaria-free reserves (Madikwe, Pilanesberg, Eastern Cape, Waterberg), KwaZulu-Natal (Hluhluwe-iMfolozi, iSimangaliso, Phinda/Nambiti), and green-season / shoulder months.

## 3. Product regions

### 3.1 Greater Kruger and the private reserves (Sabi Sand, Timbavati, Manyeleti, Klaserie etc.)

What it is: the premium safari core. Sabi Sand shares a 50 km unfenced border with Kruger National Park and is privately owned. Sources: https://www.go2africa.com/destinations/sabi-sands-game-reserve/why-go

Supply constraints:
- Sabi Sand is "in very high demand, especially during peak season"; prime lodges "booked up months in advance" (Go2Africa, same URL).
- Operator guidance on lead time: 10-12 months for June-September, festive season and flagship suites at Singita, MalaMala, Londolozi, Royal Malewane (https://www.privatetourscapetown.com/blog/private-safari-kruger-national-park). Another operator says 6-9 months for May-September (https://ellamckendrick.com/7-day-south-africa-safari-itinerary/). Both are seller blogs, so treat as directional.
- Flagship lodges "often appear fully booked on public booking sites" but "hold inventory back for local tour operators at preferential rates" (ellamckendrick.com URL above). Implication: access depends on relationships and contracts, not online search.
- Indicative price band: US$600 to US$2,500+ per person per night all-inclusive in Greater Kruger private reserves (privatetourscapetown.com, seller blog; unverified).
- Peak season is the dry winter, May-September; green season November-March offers better value and newborn animals. April and October are good shoulder months. Source: privatetourscapetown.com blog.
- Budget alternative: self-drive Kruger National Park via SANParks camps, from about US$80 per night (https://www.africansafarimag.com/south-africa-safari-cost). SANParks rates are by quote/online booking: https://www.sanparks.org/parks/kruger/rates-entry-fees
- Kruger conservation fee for international adults: R602/day until 31 Oct 2026, then R692/day (children 2-11 R300 on the old tariff). Sources: https://krugercompanion.co.za/fees/ and https://www.getsafaritours.com/tanzania/guides/african-safari-cost

Health: Greater Kruger is a malaria area; some malaria-free private reserves are offered on request (privatetourscapetown.com).

### 3.2 Malaria-free safari regions (family and first-time segment)

- Named assured malaria-free areas: Eastern Cape (e.g., Shamwari, 25,000 ha), Pilanesberg, Madikwe, Waterberg. Sources: https://www.travelbutlers.com/south-africa/safari/malaria-free/ and https://www.shamwari.com/ and https://www.privatetourscapetown.com/blog/malaria-free-safari-south-africa
- The client already sells Pilanesberg (Pilanesberg + Garden Route, https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/reseforslag/pilanesberg-garden-route/) and a family trip (https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/rundresor/familjeresa-sydafrika/).
- Implication: families with small children are a growth segment where malaria-free product removes the main objection and shifts demand away from the saturated Sabi Sand peak.

### 3.3 Cape Town and Cape Winelands

- Cape Town is the dominant international spend hub (62% of international card transactions, festive season 2025). Source: HVS link in section 2.
- Hotel occupancy: city-wide average 61.7%, peaking 81.5% in February (Cape Town Cycle Tour). This comes from a Facebook group post, so it is low-reliability: https://www.facebook.com/groups/585830309496224/posts/1814885213257388/
- Room supply: Cape Town added about 720,000 available room nights and sold about 560,000 more between 2015 and 2025 (CoStar via HVS). Hotel revenue +173% over the decade, led by luxury and upper-scale (54% of revenue growth). So price, not volume, is driving growth.
- Cape Town International Airport (CTIA) "approaching capacity"; throughput planned to rise from 30 to 45 flights per hour by 2030. Source: HVS.
- Winter (April-September) air capacity into Cape Town expected +20% in 2026 (Wesgro via HVS); off-peak room-night demand 2015-2025 grew 22% in Winelands and 31% on the Garden Route. This supports selling Cape Town + safari in Swedish autumn/winter shoulder months.
- Swedish operators sell Cape Town as the core city stay (Ving, TUI, Apollo pages: https://www.ving.se/sydafrika, https://www.tui.se/resa/sydafrika/vastra-kapprovinsen/kapstaden/, https://www.apollo.se/sydafrika). Cape Town hotel-only/charter packages are therefore well covered by mass operators; the specialist advantage is in tailor-made and guided circuits.

### 3.4 Garden Route

- Route stops and distances from Cape Town: Oudtshoorn 420 km, Knysna 500 km, Plettenberg Bay 530 km. Source: https://www.privatetourscapetown.com/blog/garden-route-south-africa
- Garden Route room-night revenue grew 59% in USD 2015-2025, with demand +17% room nights and "constrained supply, particularly during peak periods". Source: HVS.
- George Airport, the main gateway, exceeded its design capacity of about 900,000 passengers a year in 2025; ACSA has announced an expansion expected to double capacity. Source: HVS. Implication: domestic flight inflow into George is tight at peak; road transfers or Port Elizabeth/Gqeberha routings may be needed in December-January.
- Festive-season readiness is a stated concern for the Garden Route National Park (SANParks post, low detail): https://www.facebook.com/South.African.National.Parks/posts/garden-route-national-park-announces-festive-season-readiness-plansthe-garden-ro/1277850131044130/

### 3.5 KwaZulu-Natal (Hluhluwe-iMfolozi, iSimangaliso, Zululand, Durban)

- Hluhluwe-iMfolozi Park: oldest proclaimed reserve in Africa, about 96,000 ha, roughly 280 km north of Durban (about 3 hours on N2); strongest white rhino viewing. Source: https://www.safari.com/hluhluwe-game-reserve-imfolozi-park
- Indicative retail pricing from a foreign aggregator: 8-day Durban + Bayala + St Lucia package from US$1,595 per person excluding flights; Bayala lodge from US$320 per person per night (malaria-free zone). Source: safari.com URL above (competitor price, not a cost basis).
- Local operator safari packages start around R3,995 per person overnight and R8,700 per person for 3 nights (https://www.hluhluwegamereserve.com/), indicating a mid-priced, fragmented local supply of many small operators.
- The client has a historic tie to KwaZulu-Natal through its Star for Life CSR project (https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/om-oss/), which is a relationship asset for KZN contracting. [ASSUMPTION: this tie can be leveraged for supplier introductions; verify with CEO.]
- KZN is under-represented in the client's visible product list (tour pages seen: Kruger, Pilanesberg, Garden Route, Cape Town, Victoria Falls/Botswana, Rovos Rail, golf). [ASSUMPTION based on website link inventory only.]

### 3.6 Adjacent products the client already sells

From the site link inventory (https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/): guided round trips (rundresor) such as "Safari, Garden Route & Kapstaden", "Sydafrikas skatter", "Mat, vin & the Big Five", "Golf, Big Five & Garden Route", family trip, Cape Town + Victoria Falls + Botswana safari, wedding/honeymoon, Rovos Rail golf trip, plus Seychelles and Mauritius, and sister brands Sodrahavsresor (soderhavsresor.se) and MICE (mice.se). ## 5. Contracting and partnership models

### 5.1 Standard trade economics (industry guidance, generic, not South Africa-specific)
From Arival (https://arival.travel/article/how-to-set-net-rates-for-tours/):
- Inbound tour operators / DMCs: 25-30% off retail rack to arrive at net.
- Wholesalers/OTAs: 20-25%.
- Travel agents: 10-15%.
- An ITO commonly keeps 7-10% and pays another 7-10% to a downstream agent.
Another seller site claims DMC platforms deliver 20-35% margin versus 10-15% commission (https://dmcquote.com/guides/travel-agent-commission-rates-2026), promotional and low reliability.

### 5.2 Models available to the client, ranked by control vs capital

| Model | How it works | Pros | Cons / risk | Fit for 2x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Net rate via DMC (SATSA member) | Confidential net rates per season; DMC handles transfers, guides, vehicles, park fees | Lowest capital, fast, wide product | Margin shared with DMC; lodge allocation not guaranteed; DMC may also sell direct | Core model for 2027 |
| B. Direct lodge contracts (rack less trade discount, seasonal) | Direct net contract with lodge groups with allocation/release dates | Best margin, secures peak beds | Needs volume history, deposit exposure, admin | Priority for top 10-15 lodges |
| C. Allocation / block with release | Pre-book rooms for fixed guided departures with release schedule (e.g., 60-90 days) | Guarantees supply for group departures; stable cost | Attrition risk if unsold | Fit for guided round trips |
| D. Preferred-partner / volume-commitment agreement | Annual minimum room nights in exchange for better net and priority | Unlocks flagship inventory | Commitment liability, currency exposure | Fit once pipeline is proven |
| E. Own SA entity / local ground arm | Own Pty Ltd or JV with local operator | Margin and quality control | Cost, compliance, management | Evaluate for 2028, not a Q1 2027 action |
| F. Consolidators / bedbanks (e.g., hotel wholesalers) | Dynamic rates via API | Breadth, speed | Thin margin, no peak safari allocation | Fill-in for city hotels |

[ASSUMPTION] Recommended mix by 2027 peak: A for ground, B/C/D for the top safari lodges and Cape Town hotels, F for long-tail city hotels. Rationale: allocation scarcity at flagship lodges is documented, while bedbanks do not sell the scarce inventory.

### 5.3 Contract terms to negotiate (checklist)
Rates in ZAR vs USD/EUR and the date rates are fixed; currency clause; release dates and attrition; cancellation scales; deposit schedule; commission/net structure and confidentiality (rates are confidential per Arival); conservation/levy pass-through; free-of-charge (FOC) policies for guides; child policies (important for family segment); malaria-free alternatives clause; force majeure including airspace/airline disruption; payment security (the Swedish package travel law requires protection, see section 7).

## 6. Supply constraints and risks (ranked)

1. Peak safari bed scarcity (Jun-Sep, festive): flagship Sabi Sand inventory is withheld for operators and sells 6-12 months ahead (sections 3.1). Impact: caps premium revenue growth unless allocation is secured by early 2027.
2. Air capacity from Sweden: Emirates and Qatar are about 10% of weekly flight capacity into Cape Town (Wesgro via HVS). In March 2026 Gulf carriers were "sharply curtailed due to the Iran war"; Lufthansa added Frankfurt-Cape Town flights (Travel Weekly, https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Airline-News/With-Middle-East-hubs-unavailable-airlines-add-direct-Asia-Europe-flights). HVS reports KLM and Ethiopian upsizing aircraft. Sweden has no direct flight to South Africa according to the search results, which listed one-stop options: Qatar (ARN-CPT one-way from about US$418), Lufthansa, British Airways, Emirates, KLM (https://www.google.com/travel/flights/flights-from-stockholm-to-cape-town.html; https://www.travelocity.com/lp/flight-routes/qatar-airways-from-arlanda-to-cape-town-intl/qr/arn/cpt). Details belong to research file 04-air-access.md.
3. Price inflation on supply side: luxury Cape Town ADR CAGR 8.7% (2015-2025); Kruger international fee +14.9% from 1 Nov 2026. Rand moves matter: HVS notes continued rand depreciation, which cushions USD buyers but contracts quoted in USD/EUR remove that benefit. Swedish krona to rand pass-through not researched.
4. Gateway congestion: CTIA near capacity (30 flights/hour, planned 45 by 2030); George Airport above design volume in 2025 (HVS).
5. Safety and health perception: US advises "exercise increased caution" (crime, unrest, kidnapping; https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/south-africa.html); Canada and Australia "high degree of caution" (https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/south-africa; https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/africa/south-africa). Australia notes an Ebola-related health questionnaire for arrivals from DRC or Uganda (not Sweden-relevant but signals health-event risk). Swedish UD advisory text was not retrieved. Ving's page references UD yellow-fever certificate rules for travellers from/through yellow-fever countries (https://www.ving.se/sydafrika). Impact: a disruption can reduce demand abruptly; contracts need flexible cancellation.
6. Fragmented, small-operator supply in KZN and Eastern Cape means vetting and quality-control cost.
7. Staffing: product and contracting capacity at the client are limited (about 8 named specialists plus guides on the site's team page, https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/om-oss/). Doubling pax with the same contracting headcount is unlikely. [ASSUMPTION: team page reflects current staffing.]

## 8. Implications for doubling revenue (40 to 80 MSEK in 24 months)

Illustrative revenue-per-booking math. All figures [ASSUMPTION] because average package price and pax are unknown.

| Average revenue per traveller (SEK) | Travellers for 40 MSEK | Travellers for 80 MSEK |
|---|---|---|
| 35,000 | 1,143 | 2,286 |
| 50,000 | 800 | 1,600 |
| 70,000 | 571 | 1,143 |

Reading: if the real average is near the middle row, the plan needs about 800 more travellers a year. If supply-side inflation lifts average price by 8-9% a year (the Cape Town luxury ADR trend), 2 years of mix and price drift alone could provide roughly 17-19% of the uplift [ASSUMPTION - calculated as 1.085^2 - 1 = 17.7%; not a forecast]. The rest must come from volume.

Supply actions that follow:
1. Lock peak 2027 and 2028 allocations at 10-12 months before arrival. For June-September 2027 departures the practical window is already closing: it is 2 Nov 2026 and operators advise 10-12 months. Priority 1 for Q4 2026.
2. Build a tiered lodge portfolio: Tier 1 flagship (5-8 lodges, relationship contracts), Tier 2 mid-priced reliable (15-20), Tier 3 value/family/malaria-free (10-15). [ASSUMPTION: counts are planning targets, not market facts.]
3. Shift part of demand to shoulder and green season (Oct-Apr) and to malaria-free regions to avoid peak scarcity; Winter air capacity into Cape Town is increasing 20% in 2026.
4. Develop fixed-departure guided tours with block allocations; this is the most supply-controllable way to add volume at known cost.
5. Add KZN and Eastern Cape product to broaden beyond Kruger/Cape Town, and package honeymoon/golf/rail niches already on the site.
6. Contract in ZAR where possible or hedge; build a currency clause and annual price-review calendar.
7. Secure at least two air-access paths (Gulf carrier + European carrier via FRA/AMS/LHR) in the product design to reduce airspace-disruption risk.
8. Add a DMC panel of 3-5 SATSA-vetted partners to avoid single-point dependency; start formal supplier scorecards (service, safety, claims, payment terms).
9. Add contracting capacity: at least one dedicated product/contracting manager and a South Africa-based representative or DMC liaison. [ASSUMPTION]
10. Coordinate with the MICE unit (mice.se is a linked sister site) to reuse group allocations in shoulder months for incentive/conference groups. [ASSUMPTION: shared contracting is feasible.]
