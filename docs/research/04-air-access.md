# 04 - Air Access: Sweden/Nordics to South Africa

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: airlines, routings, capacity, fare seasonality, new routes, risks, and what they mean for doubling sydafrikaresor.se turnover (40 -> 80 MSEK by end 2028).
Convention: sourced facts carry a URL. Anything not sourced is marked [ASSUMPTION]. Company-internal data is unknown and is not invented.

## 1. Headline findings

1. There is no nonstop flight from Sweden (ARN/GOT) or Denmark (CPH) to South Africa. Every Swedish customer connects at least once. Sources: Skyscanner, Booking.com, flightconnections, Momondo (all say "no direct"):
   - https://www.skyscanner.com/routes/cpt/arn/cape-town-to-stockholm-arlanda.html
   - https://www.booking.com/flights/route/city-to-country/se-stockholm-to-za.html
   - https://www.momondo.se/flyg/arlanda-airport-arn/sydafrika
2. Total elapsed time Stockholm-Cape Town is about 15 h 10 min (Flygresor.se; Göteborg 15 h 14 min, Köpenhamn 16 h 20 min) and KLM via Amsterdam about 14 h 55 min. Source: https://www.flygresor.se/sydafrika/kapstaden and https://www.klm.se/sv-se/flyg-fran-stockholm-till-kapstaden
3. Cape Town (not Johannesburg) is where capacity is growing fastest. Winter/peak 2026/27: about +250,000 two-way international seats (+12%), 26 international airlines, 33 destinations, 38 routes, 255 international flights per week. Source (ACSA): https://www.airports.co.za/news?item=447
4. Hub-and-spoke competition for Swedish passengers is strong: KLM (Amsterdam), Lufthansa Group (Frankfurt/Munich, Swiss/Edelweiss Zurich), Air France (Paris), Turkish (Istanbul), Qatar/Emirates (Doha/Dubai), plus British Airways/Virgin (London) and Ethiopian (Addis) [last two: ASSUMPTION that they are sold from Sweden, not verified].
5. Gulf hubs carry a live 2026 risk: US-Israel/Iran conflict from 28 Feb 2026 closed UAE/Qatar/Bahrain/Kuwait airspace for about a week; Emirates still at about 75% capacity in June; Middle East carriers forecast a net loss of $4.3bn in 2026 (IATA). Sources: https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/middle-eastern-airlines-lose-43bn-2026-amid-iran-war and https://www.reuters.com/world/china/airline-shares-rebound-trickle-middle-east-flights-resume-2026-03-05/
6. Jet fuel is a fare driver: IATA forecasts the 2026 jet fuel average about 70% above 2025 (via Middle East Eye, same link). Lufthansa announced cancelling 20,000 flights through Oct 2026, mostly short-haul (https://resources.radiantdelivers.com/me-disruptions-26-age).

## 3. Capacity and demand backdrop

- Cape Town International (CTIA): about 11.1M two-way passengers in 2025, +7% y/y (ACSA link). July 2026: 242,077 two-way international passengers, +10% y/y (ATTA link).
- 2026/27 peak season: +250,000 international seats (+12%), 255 weekly international flights (ACSA link).
- South Africa total: 10.5M international visitors in 2025 (record; up from 8.92M in 2024) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_South_Africa ). Europe arrivals up 12.6% in 2025, with Sweden named among the growth markets (https://www.instagram.com/p/DUaIO-eDQ6w/ - low-confidence social repost of Zawya). August 2026 arrivals 1,005,286, +7.4% y/y (https://www.riotimesonline.com/south-africa-tourist-arrivals-fall-16-9-percent-2026/ ).
- Swedish arrivals to SA: only an unreliable comment ("around 20K") was found. The Stats SA 2025 release says 213,138 tourists from a group of countries were 8.9% of overseas arrivals, but the group is not identified on the page I could read (https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19362 ). Treat Swedish volume as UNKNOWN; obtain from South African Tourism / Stats SA table "Tourism and Migration" by country.
- Implication for doubling: supply growth into CPT (+12% seats) is roughly 1/8 of the +100% revenue needed, so growth must come from share gain, higher yield per traveler and longer/combined itineraries, not from market tailwind alone.

## 4. Fare seasonality (what we know)

- KLM published lowest return fares ARN-CPT: SEK 6,723 (Nov-Dec 2026), SEK 6,843 (Jan 2027); Feb-Apr 2027 not shown (no data). Source: KLM link above. Note: these are "from" fares, not what packages carry.
- Flygresor.se: "from SEK 6,008" and cheapest month September (about SEK 7,666 average) - https://www.flygresor.se/sydafrika/kapstaden . Momondo shows from SEK 3,270 one-way [indicative marketing "from" price].
- Emirates Business from SEK 42,142 (Emirates link) - indicates a 6x spread between economy low and premium.
- Seasonality of demand [ASSUMPTION, based on South African seasons, to be validated with internal booking data]: Swedish peak is Nov-Mar (SA summer and Swedish winter, Christmas/New Year, sportlov Feb-Mar, påsk), Sept-Oct and Apr-May for wildlife/Garden Route/wine shoulder periods; Jun-Aug SA winter is the weakest for Cape Town but strong for safari. Fares peak around Swedish school holidays and Christmas, which collide with the Cape Town peak seat increase.
- Seasonal nonstops from European hubs (Condor, Edelweiss, Norse, Air France, Virgin) operate mainly in the SA summer window (roughly Sept/Oct-Mar/Apr), so Nov-Mar has the widest choice and the highest fares.

## 6. Risks

1. Gulf hub disruption / war risk: renewed closures would strand packages routed via DXB/DOH. EASA advised avoiding Bahrain/Kuwait/Qatar/UAE airspace until 31 Aug 2026 (cited in Middle East Eye). European carriers (Lufthansa, Air France, BA) were still partially suspended to some Gulf points. Mitigation: dual-routing capability, insurance, and clear terms.
2. Fuel and fares: jet fuel forecast about +70% in 2026; about 70% of surcharges passed to consumers (McKinsey via MEE). Price-sensitive leisure demand may fall; yield per booking rises but conversion drops.
3. Concentration: Swedish customers depend on 1-stop partners; KLM and Turkish each now run 10 weekly CPT flights, creating a bottleneck at peak (Dec-Jan, sportlov). Seat availability and group-block rules matter more than list fare.
4. Airline distress: low-cost carriers under stress (Spirit ceased 2 May 2026; airBaltic and Wizz at risk per MEE). airBaltic is relevant for Nordic feed [ASSUMPTION: only if used by competitors].
5. Taxes: UK APD increase on Africa flights from April 2028 affects London routings (ATTA link).
6. Health/entry: US CDC Ebola attestation (Nov 2026) is US-bound only but signals outbreak-driven rule changes (ATTA listing). No SA-specific measure found.
7. South Africa-specific operational risks (load-shedding at airports, ground handling, security, fuel supply) not researched in depth - UNKNOWN.

## 7. Implications for the 40 -> 80 MSEK plan

- Revenue doubling by capacity logic: if average package price is flat, the number of travelers must double. If the average rises through longer trips, more safari, premium cabins and add-ons, travelers grow less. [ASSUMPTION] A mix of about +50% travelers and about +33% yield reaches 2x; test against actual data.
- Air supply is not the binding constraint on Cape Town (+12% seats) but seat inventory at the right price in Dec-Mar may be. Action: secure group/block (GDS "fare basis" and group contract) allocations with KLM, Turkish, Qatar and Lufthansa Group for Nov 2027-Mar 2028 by Q1 2027.
- Product design to exploit new capacity: open-jaw (fly into CPT, out of JNB/DUR) enabled by Turkish/Qatar/Emirates multi-city; CPT arrival via Condor/Edelweiss from Frankfurt/Zurich with Swedish feeders; add Airlink Mauritius/Zanzibar beach extensions.
- Nordic catchment: offer CPH/GOT departures (Göteborg 15 h 14 min per Flygresor) in addition to ARN; Qatar and KLM both reachable from Nordic cities.
- Shoulder-season shift: push Sept-Oct and Apr-May to lift off-peak load factor when fares are lower (cheapest month September per Flygresor).
- Contingency messaging: "fly via Europe or Gulf" choice, with an Emirates-style disruption guarantee as a competitive benchmark (MEE). Competitors will use it; the agency should check whether supplier insurance covers war-related cancellation.
- Distribution: build an NDC/GDS fare watch on ARN/GOT/CPH-CPT/JNB for 2027-2028 and publish "air + land" packages with fare-lock dates.
- Owner (by role): Product/Air Desk Manager owns routing and block strategy; Revenue/Finance owns margin model; CEO sponsors supplier negotiations.
