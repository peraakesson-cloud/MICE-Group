## 1. Executive findings

1. Public benchmarks for travel margins are weak and mostly US/tour-activity oriented. The only hard Nordic anchor is Vinggruppen (Nordic Leisure Travel Group): revenue 16,799 MSEK, EBITDA 953 MSEK, pre-tax margin 4.2% (FY Oct 2024 - Sep 2025). Large-scale charter packages run on thin net margins, so specialist long-haul must earn its keep on gross margin per booking, not volume alone.
2. The brand's own site lists "from" prices of roughly SEK 17,900 to 95,200 per person for trips of 10-21 days. Published guided round trips cluster at SEK 44,300 - 69,900. Competitor "from" prices for South Africa sit at about SEK 25,000 - 37,400 pp. Revenue growth therefore comes from bookings x party size x average price, and the mix of guided round trips (higher ticket) versus tailor-made (lower "from" price) is the main lever.
3. Swedish regulation changed on 1 April 2026: a collective travel guarantee fund now takes a 0.6% levy on the price of every package travel (bookings made from 1 April 2026), while the individual guarantee shrinks over about 5 years toward 6.25% of guarantee-liable annual turnover. Doubling turnover raises both the cash tied up in guarantees and the levy (about 0.24 MSEK at 40 MSEK to 0.48 MSEK at 80 MSEK, levy only).
4. Currency is the largest controllable margin risk. SEK/ZAR was about 1.696 on 2 Nov 2026 and is down 7% over 12 months, i.e. the rand strengthened about 7.5% against the krona. Swedish law only allows a price increase if the contract says so, the increase is notified at least 20 days before departure, and the traveller may cancel free if the increase exceeds 8%. The company's own terms add a 100 SEK per booking threshold. That means FX risk between booking and ~20 days before departure largely sits with the operator unless hedged or priced in.
5. Deposit/cash-flow model is favourable: the brand's published terms take a 3,000 SEK registration fee within 10 days of confirmation, and a stepped cancellation scale (5,000 SEK pp before 60 days; 40% at 59-46 days; 75% at 45-31 days; 100% inside 30 days). Customer cash arrives far earlier than supplier cash for many components, which funds growth, but only if the guarantee is sized for it and FX is hedged against the cash held.

### 2.2 Two business models to keep separate in the plan

- **Principal / package organiser (paketresa):** books ground and air components, sells at a package price, recognises gross revenue (or net, depending on accounting policy [ASSUMPTION: confirm with the company's accountant]). Margin = package price minus supplier costs. Legal obligations: package travel law, travel guarantee, price-change rules.
- **Agent / intermediary (commission):** earns 10-20% commission on flights/hotels/insurance sold on behalf of others. Lower risk, lower margin, and "linked travel arrangements" (sammanlänkade researrangemang) still trigger a partial guarantee obligation (https://www.kammarkollegiet.se/vara-tjanster/resegaranti/fragor-och-svar-om-resegaranti-for-reseaktorer).

The public site calls itself "Sveriges största researrangör till södra Afrika" and states it issues full travel guarantees to Kammarkollegiet (https://www.sydafrikaresor.se/), which indicates the principal/organiser model for packages.

### 2.3 Gross turnover vs gross profit (key point for the 40 to 80 MSEK goal)

The 80 MSEK target is a turnover goal. At an **[ASSUMPTION] 18-25% gross margin on turnover** (rationale: multi-day tour benchmark 25-35% lowered for flight pass-through at low margin, and Vinggruppen's thin net margin as a floor), 40 MSEK of turnover is about 7.2-10 MSEK gross profit and 80 MSEK is about 14.4-20 MSEK. Overhead and marketing must fit inside that increment of 7-10 MSEK. See section 7 for the marketing-budget implication.

### 4.3 Legal framework

- Price changes: contract must allow it; permitted for changes in exchange rates, fuel costs, taxes/fees; notify >=20 days before departure with calculation; traveller may cancel if rise exceeds 8% https://www.konsumentverket.se/lagar/paketreselagen-konsument/ ; also Paketreselag 2018:1217 https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/paketreselag-20181217_sfs-2018-1217/ .
- Extraordinary circumstances: full refund if unavoidable and extraordinary events affect the destination (Konsumentverket link above).
- Travel guarantee (Kammarkollegiet, page updated 2026-09-23): https://www.kammarkollegiet.se/vara-tjanster/resegaranti/fragor-och-svar-om-resegaranti-for-reseaktorer
  - Minimum individual guarantee 50,000 SEK; sized on expected package revenue for the next 12 months; may be bank/insurance guarantee, deposited funds, etc.
  - Fund levy: 0.6% of the price of every package travel (bookings from 1 April 2026); report and pay three times a year (31 May / 30 Sep / 31 Jan reporting; 10 Jun / 10 Oct / 10 Feb payment).
  - Reform context: fund target 1.5bn SEK over about 5 years; individual guarantees shrink toward 6.25% of guarantee-liable turnover. Source: Visita https://visita.se/viktiga-forandringar-i-resegarantilagen/ (published 2026-02-26).

Individual guarantee at steady state (6.25% of liable turnover) = 2.5 MSEK at 40 MSEK, 5.0 MSEK at 80 MSEK, and higher in the transition years. Sensitivity formula: margin impact = (share of cost in foreign currency) x (FX move). **[ASSUMPTION] 55-65% of cost of goods is ZAR/USD/EUR-linked.** With an assumed 20% gross margin, a 10% adverse move on 60% foreign-currency cost removes about 6 points of margin - roughly 30% of gross profit - before any price increase right. Sensitivity of 80 MSEK of turnover: about 4.8 MSEK of gross profit per 10% move on that cost share (own calculation, assumption-driven).

### 5.3 Hedging toolbox (generic)

- Forward contracts lock rates for 3 days to 1 year and suit known, dated supplier payments (https://www.trade.gov/foreign-exchange-risk ; https://convera.com/blog/cross-border-payments/currency-forward-contract-business-fx-risk-management/ ). Trade-off: no upside participation; may require margin/deposit.
- Options give protection with upside but cost a premium (same Convera source).
- Natural hedges: collect customer deposits and pay suppliers in matching timing; buy foreign currency when the 40% instalment is received; negotiate SEK or EUR rate cards for flights.
- Selling-price tools: publish prices with a stated FX reference rate and a contractual price-adjustment clause (within the 8%/20-day/100 SEK rules).

### 5.4 Recommended policy for doubling (proposal, to be validated by the finance function and the bank)

1. Hedge ratio: 50-70% of known ZAR/USD supplier payments for the next 6-12 months, layered monthly (rolling forward book), rather than all at once [ASSUMPTION - policy design, no source].
2. Trigger hedges at time of booking confirmation (when the sales price is fixed), sized to the cost-of-goods foreign component of that booking.
3. Review the FX buffer in the price list quarterly; start at a 3-5% embedded buffer plus contractual adjustment clause [ASSUMPTION].
4. Treat hedging as budget certainty, not speculation; with a doubled book of business the notional hedge volume could exceed 30-40 MSEK per year [ASSUMPTION: 55% x 80 MSEK].

## 7. Implications for doubling revenue (40 to 80 MSEK in 24 months)

1. **Growth math:** base case needs ~400 more bookings per year by 2028 (section 3.2) while keeping margin. Every 1 point of gross margin on 80 MSEK is 0.8 MSEK.
2. **Marketing budget envelope [ASSUMPTION]:** an incremental 40 MSEK turnover at 20% gross margin yields 8 MSEK of gross profit. If acquisition cost is held at 6-8% of turnover (about 2.4-3.2 MSEK incremental ad/marketing spend per year at the 80 MSEK run-rate) the incremental net contribution stays positive. The AtlasPerk page quotes marketing at about 18% of revenue for tour operators - far above that - so verify the real figure; if CAC is near 12-18% of turnover, doubling does not pay without ABV/margin improvement.
3. **Price over discount:** a 10% price rise gives 25-40% profit growth and a 10% discount can destroy 50-70% of profit (CaptainBook via AtlasPerk). Do not buy volume with price cuts; favour packaging, upgrades and early-booking incentives funded from supplier rebates.
4. **Mix:** guided round trips (44,300 - 69,900 pp) carry roughly 1.5x the price of tailor-made entry itineraries (17,900 - 31,700 pp). Moving mix up is cheaper than adding bookings.
5. **Working capital:** deposits and instalments fund suppliers; larger turnover raises guarantee size and costs, the fund levy (0.6%), and FX hedge notional. Budget for 2.5-5.0 MSEK of guarantee capacity at steady state plus transition-period amounts [ASSUMPTION-based].
6. **Regulatory tailwind/headwind:** guarantee reform lowers long-run individual guarantee cost for small operators and improves trust marketing; the 0.6% levy is a new, unavoidable cost of about 0.24 MSEK extra from doubling.
7. **FX:** a stronger rand cuts margin or forces list-price increases of 3-6%; plan a pricing review before each season and publish prices in SEK with a transparent clause.
