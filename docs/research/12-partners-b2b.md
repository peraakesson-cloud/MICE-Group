# 12. Growth Partnerships and B2B2C Channels (South Africa leisure, Sweden)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: South African Tourism (SAT), airlines, destination/tourism boards, co-marketing, affinity groups, B2B2C channels, Swedish travel fairs.
Goal context: sydafrikaresor.se from SEK 40 M to SEK 80 M annual turnover within 24 months (calendar 2027 and 2028).

Convention: every fact below has a source URL. Anything without a source is tagged **[ASSUMPTION]** with a rationale and is collected in section 9 so the CEO can replace it with internal data. No internal company data was available; no competitor revenue or booking figures are stated.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. The destination is in a tailwind. South Africa had a record 10.48 M international arrivals in 2025 (+17.6%); Sweden grew +12.5% and SAT calls Europe "a critical growth engine" (SAT press release [S1]). Absolute Swedish arrivals were not found in public sources checked (unknown U1).
2. SAT runs Sweden through a "North Europe Hub" that works through trade partners: in 2025 it ran a trade marketing initiative "together with 15 of North Europe's top Africa sellers" [S1]. Getting sydafrikaresor.se formally into that group is the single highest-leverage partnership move, because it unlocks co-funded campaigns, training, FAM trips and SAT content.
3. Airlift is the main external risk and a negotiable asset. Gulf carriers cut flights sharply in spring 2026 (Emirates -34%, Qatar -56% in April 2026 versus a year earlier, Cirium data via Travel Weekly [S6]); Qatar is now rebuilding (Johannesburg 14 to 18 weekly from 2 Dec 2026 [S5]). Turkish Airlines shows ARN-JNB from SEK 6,004 and ARN-CPT from SEK 6,502 return for late-Nov 2026 dates, and ARN-CPT from SEK 6,548 for Feb/Apr 2027 dates [S7]. Airline co-op/fare deals and block seats are a margin and conversion lever.
4. The Swedish retail landscape is fragmented (IBISWorld: no company above 5% share [S12]), and larger mass-market players (Ving, TUI/Fritidsresor, Kilroy) already sell South Africa [S13-S15]. Sydafrikaresor.se positions itself as the specialist ("Skandinaviens största arrangör av resor till Sydafrika och södra Afrika", since 2003) [S11]. B2B2C should therefore be a specialist-wholesale and co-brand play, not a mass-market price play.
5. Doubling needs roughly 2x passengers at constant average price. Using the site's published "from" prices (SEK 17,900 to 95,200 per person [S11]) and an **[ASSUMPTION]** blended net revenue of about SEK 40,000 per passenger, SEK 40 M is about 1,000 passengers and SEK 80 M about 2,000. Partnerships are proposed to carry **[ASSUMPTION]** 20-30% of the incremental SEK 40 M (SEK 8-12 M, roughly 200-300 passengers) by end of 2028; the rest must come from owned digital, paid media and repeat/referral (covered by other research files).

## 3. South African Tourism (SAT) and destination-side partners

### 3.1 What SAT does in the Nordics (sourced)
- Sweden falls under the **North Europe Hub** (Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden named in the 2025 release). Hub activities in 2025: trade marketing with 15 top Africa sellers, presence at Vakantiebeurs (NL) and a seven-city "Come Find Your Joy" roadshow connecting South African product owners with 240 trade partners across NL and BE [S1].
- SAT's trade website lists country sections for the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain and others, with a Sweden "Know your Swedish visitor" page and fact sheet PDF but no Sweden-specific trade office listing on the pages checked [S4]. This suggests Sweden is served remotely or via an agency. A 2019 case study shows SAT used an agency (United Spirit Nordic) for "full representation in the Nordics": 8 training sessions in 8 cities (avg 15 agents each), 3 roadshows, a MEGA FAM (552 agents invited, 82 travelled, 47 first-timers), 190+ agents trained, Nordic arrivals +5% in 2019 [S8]. That arrangement may be outdated; current Nordic representation is unknown (U2).
- SAT markets trade tools: Welcome toolkit, banners, fact sheet for Sweden [S4]; consumer content "Come Find Your Joy" is used in other markets [S1].

Implications:
- No Swedish nonstop to South Africa was found in the searches; all options are one-stop (Doha, Istanbul, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Addis etc.). The one-stop hub mix is changing in 2026-27: recovery of Gulf capacity (Qatar +29% on JNB weekly frequency per [S5]) versus European and Turkish carriers that picked up demand [S6]. Industry analyst quoted in [S6] expects a fare war when Gulf capacity fully returns, which is an opportunity to lock lower land-plus-air package prices in 2027.
- Airfare of roughly SEK 6,000-6,500 is a small share of a SEK 17,900-57,900 land-heavy itinerary [S11], so the agency's value is in land content, not air. Partnerships with airlines should therefore focus on (a) trade fares/ NDC deals, (b) co-op marketing budgets, (c) schedule intelligence, (d) group/ block space for guided tours, rather than air margin.

## 8. Implications for doubling revenue

1. **Order of attack** (impact vs effort): (a) SAT North Europe Hub membership and co-op funds; (b) airline trade/co-op deals and block seats; (c) senior/affinity partnerships plus Resemässan/Seniorfestivalen presence; (d) agent portal and operator land-only deals; (e) TMC/ corporate bleisure through the MICE unit.
2. **Contribution plan (illustrative; replace with internal numbers)**: of the incremental SEK 40 M, partnerships and B2B2C are planned to deliver SEK 8-12 M by FY2028 (2027: SEK 3-5 M; 2028: SEK 8-12 M run-rate). At **[ASSUMPTION]** SEK 40,000 blended revenue per passenger that is 200-300 passengers of the roughly 1,000 extra needed.
3. **Budget envelope for the partnership workstream (24 months)**: **[ASSUMPTION]** SEK 1.4-2.6 M, including fairs (SEK 0.4-0.8 M), co-op marketing (SEK 0.6-1.2 M own share), FAM and content (SEK 0.3-0.5 M), agent portal/training (about SEK 0.1-0.2 M). Target blended partner-acquisition cost below 6% of partner-generated revenue **[ASSUMPTION]**.
4. **Seasonality alignment**: build partner campaigns for booking windows Aug-Nov (Dec-Mar travel) and Jan-Mar (Easter and autumn departures), consistent with Stats SA seasonality [S3].
5. **Risk hedges**: airline capacity volatility [S6]; currency (SEK/ZAR) and fuel-driven fare rises; dependency on a few suppliers; need for supply capacity (lodges, guides) to take 2x volume (see file 05-product-supply.md).
