## 2. Principles

1. **Hire behind evidence, but never behind the queue.** Hires 2+ are released by gates (strategy section 13.3 and 16.2). Variable capacity (section 7) covers the gap between a gate decision and productive hires, so marketing spend is not frozen waiting for people.
2. **Reduce workload before hiring.** Itinerary library, quote templates, automated cadences, standardised booking files (strategy 13.3, WS14). The assumed 3 h per quote and 8 h per booking are illustrative [A23]; measure the real figure in the first 60 days.
3. **Route by strength.** Top leads go to the best converting consultants for that trip type (WS14 routing). Reported conversion variance of 2-3x between agents (R11, a Rework vendor guide, tier C, directional) suggests coaching and routing can be cheaper than a hire, but this is unverified; test with own data before relying on it.
4. **Pay for margin, service and retention of customers, not for turnover.** Pure revenue pay creates discounting and weak files, and breaks gate G1.
5. **Buy what is spiky or specialised, build what is the moat.** Customer conversation, product knowledge, supply contracting, CRM ownership and brand voice stay in-house. Paid media operation, specialist technical SEO, video production and peak overflow can be bought.
6. **Shared pools are not free.** The SA team shares people with Pacific, Australia/NZ and MICE. The southern-hemisphere summer peak overlaps all of them (strategy 13.3). A shared capacity calendar and escalation rules come before any hire is justified.
7. **The CEO is new.** The plan gives the CEO a short list of people decisions with dates and avoids a reorganisation in the first 90 days beyond naming owners.

Reading: +3.5 consultant-equivalents of handling work is covered by 3 consultants, 1 booking-ops coordinator (who takes document and payment admin off consultants, which is why it counts as capacity) and automation. The product and contracting manager is not handling capacity: it addresses allocation, DMC, guide and supply-readiness workload (strategy 13.2, 13.5-13.7) that has no owner at 22 staff [ASSUMPTION]. At stretch the sixth FTE is a fourth consultant or a customer-care/in-trip lead (decision at Gate 3).

**Capacity guard (G9):** after the first 60-day baseline the Head of Sales sets a maximum of active open quotes per consultant. WS14 plans about 25 quotes per consultant-month as a proxy (derived). When the cap is hit, marketing scale-ups pause and variable capacity (section 7) is triggered first, not marketing cuts.

**Cost check on a single consultant hire:** SEK 0.7 M loaded [A18]. At 20% gross margin [A7] a consultant breaks even at SEK 3.5 M of turnover handled, about 35 bookings at SEK 101k per booking (strategy arithmetic: 45,000 x 2.2 travellers). Planned load is far above that, so the constraint is ramp time and supply, not the unit economics of the hire. The programme as a whole is net negative at 20% margin (strategy section 12.2), so the hire plan is protected by the stop-loss in section 11, not by a business case that assumes more margin.

## 9. Incentives and retention

### 9.1 Design principles

1. Reward **order-book margin**, response SLA, review score and conversion, in that order (WS14 point 10).
2. Share between team and individual: team bonus builds cooperation across consultants and booking-ops; individual component rewards conversion and CRM hygiene.
3. No payment on discounted bookings below floors; commission-like pay is calculated on realised gross profit, after discount, so incentives align with G1 (20% blended, 18% on ladder entry, 15% after partner commission).
4. Clawback or deferral: a share is paid at departure or after the trip (service quality), not at booking, to align with cancellation and complaint risk [ASSUMPTION].
5. Compliance with Swedish employment law and any collective agreement for the sector; HR and union reviews before communication [VERIFY with HR which agreement applies].
6. Variable pay is **additional** to the 0.7 M loaded cost per FTE [A18, O-A3] and is financed from realised margin (the pool pays only when margin is at or above floor); the CFO confirms affordability by 31 Jan 2027.

### 9.2 Proposed structure for consultants (illustrative; HR and CFO to finalise)

| Component | Weight of variable pay | Metric | Gate / guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team margin pool | 40% | Gross margin of bookings departing (or paid) in the period vs the plan (20% blended, G1) | Pool zero if team margin under 18%; 50% payout at 18%, 100% at 20% or above, linear in between [ASSUMPTION] |
| Service gate | 20% | SLA first reply under 1 h for 90%; Trustpilot rolling at 4.2+; complaints per 1,000 within limit | If SLA or rating missed, this component is zero and the margin pool halves |
