## 1. Summary

1. **This workstream is a trust and differentiation layer, not a volume engine.** The strategy credits no order-book lever to sustainability. It supports L5 (PR, creators), L9 (new community-based products), L8 (B2B and corporate buyers who increasingly ask for a sustainability policy [ASSUMPTION SU2]) and the premium positioning. We assign it no revenue target. We measure it on proof assets, claim compliance and indirect indicators (section 10).
2. **The legal context changed five weeks ago.** The EU Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EU) 2024/825 applies from 27 September 2026. It prohibits generic environmental claims ("miljövänlig", "klimatvänlig", "hållbar resa", "grön") when recognised excellent environmental performance cannot be shown, unless the specification is given in clear and prominent terms on the same medium, and it targets self-created or self-certified sustainability labels (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj/eng ; https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-new-rules-on-green-claims-apply-from-27-september-2026). Konsumentverket's page states that it awaits Swedish national legislation that is "not yet decided" and that it supervises misleading environmental claims under Marknadsföringslagen without pre-approving material (https://www.konsumentverket.se/marknadsratt-foretag/miljopastaenden-regler-for-foretag/). The Swedish transposition status must be re-checked on the day we start [VERIFY]. Our rule does not depend on it: **no generic green claim, ever; only specific, evidenced, dated, same-place-qualified claims.**
3. **Recommended internal positioning: "Ansvarsfullt resande, bevisat i detalj", not "hållbar resa".** This is an internal theme line, not a public headline: used publicly it would be a class A claim (section 4.2); public copy uses only the specific proof points. We say what we do (who we buy from, how local people benefit, what a guide earns, what we measure) and what we do not claim (we do not claim a "climate-neutral" or "eco" trip). South Africa leisure travel is long-haul flight dominated, so any claim that suggests low climate impact would be misleading and is excluded.
4. **Three-tier certification path, low cost first** (section 6): Tier 1 (now): adopt a written Responsible Travel Policy aligned to the GSTC Tour Operator Standard (public domain, https://www.gstc.org/for-tour-operators/); Tier 2 (2027): join Travelife for Tour Operators and Travel Agents at "Engaged" and work to "Partner"/"Certified" (3-stage programme; GSTC status of the Tour Operator scheme to be confirmed on gstc.org before any statement, see 5.2; https://www.travelife.info/home/ ; https://www.gstc.org/for-tour-operators/); Tier 3 (supply side): prefer and mark suppliers certified by Fair Trade Tourism (independent programme for southern Africa, https://www.threetreehill.co.za/pages/fair-trade-tourism-2/ ; https://destinet.eu/resources/certificates/fair-trade-tourism) or other credible schemes. **We display a certification only after the certificate is issued and the logo-use terms are read.**
5. **Budget is small and takes no new marketing money**: SEK 20,000 (Q4 2026), SEK 185,000 (2027), SEK 165,000 (2028) cash, carved out of existing strategy lines (SEO and content, partnerships, measurement) and the Head of Product budget, which is not a strategy marketing line and must be confirmed (section 11). Staff time is about 0.25 FTE, roughly SEK 175,000 a year at the strategy's SEK 0.7 M loaded FTE [A18]. The strategy's incremental FTE are named roles (consultants, booking-ops, product/contracting manager) and do not include this time, so it is a hidden cost carried by existing staff, in competition with strategy risk R1 (capacity). Certification fees are unknown and shown as a range [ASSUMPTION SU3]. See section 11.
6. **Biggest risks**: greenwashing exposure under the new rules (a single sloppy ad can cost more than the programme), a claim that outruns what suppliers actually do, and the programme becoming a cost without a commercial return. Stop rules are in section 13.

## 3. Strategic approach

### 3.1 Role in the strategy
- **Defence of the premium.** The strategy positions the company 15-20% above the group-with-flights cluster. Responsible-travel substance (named local partners, fair guide pay, community experiences) is part of the inclusions-and-guide story that justifies the premium, instead of discounting.
- **Product differentiation (L9).** Community-based experiences (township and rural experiences run by local owners, local-guide-led cultural and heritage visits, conservation experiences with a verifiable community or conservation link) are a content and itinerary theme within existing South Africa/southern Africa coverage, not a separate business line.
- **B2B and corporate (L8, GL2).** Corporate and MICE buyers often require a supplier sustainability policy in tenders [ASSUMPTION SU2]. A published policy and Travelife status is a ticket to play and costs little.
- **Risk reduction.** A disciplined claims register protects against Konsumentverket attention, ARN complaints and social-media greenwashing call-outs, which are costly for a brand selling on trust.

### 3.2 Positioning statement (internal)
"We take responsibility for how our trips affect the people and places we send guests to. We say only what we can prove, we publish what we do and what we have not yet solved, and we help the customer choose experiences where local people own and earn from the tourism."

Principles:
1. **Specific beats general.** "Besök familjen X:s kooperativ i Y; 100% av avgiften går till kooperativet [if verified]" beats "ansvarsfull turism".
2. **Honest about flights.** Long-haul flights are the largest emissions item for most itineraries [ASSUMPTION SU5: typical share not computed]. We never imply otherwise. We offer information and choices (stay longer, fewer internal flights, rail/road alternatives where feasible, direct versus connecting flights), not offsets sold as neutrality.
3. **Social first, then environmental.** Our strongest credible story in South Africa is people: guides, local owners, fair wages, community involvement. Environmental claims stay narrow and measured.
4. **Customer value first.** Responsible-travel content is useful travel content (e.g., where to meet local makers), not a lecture.
5. **No ranking of customers' choices.** No shaming; no "green" upsell pressure.

### 3.3 What we will not do
- Use "klimatneutral", "koldioxidneutral", "miljövänlig", "grön", "eko", "hållbar", "ansvarsfull" as unqualified headline claims.
- Show an offsetting scheme as a reason to claim a neutral trip. (The Directive also targets claims that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive impact on the environment based on offsetting; see the Directive text above [VERIFY exact provision with counsel].)
- Create our own "eco-label" or badge (self-certified labels are targeted by the rules).
- Show a third-party logo before the certificate is issued.
- Use charity ("vi stöder lokalsamhället") as a sales hook without a named beneficiary, a stated amount or share, and a payment trail.
- Voluntourism with children (orphanage visits, school visits for tourists). Excluded from the portfolio [ASSUMPTION SU6: product decision to confirm; consistent with widely published child-protection guidance].
- Wildlife encounters involving captive-animal contact (cub petting, walking with lions, elephant riding). Excluded or flagged in supplier screening [ASSUMPTION SU6].

## 4. Claims discipline (core control, build first)

### 4.1 Claims register
One shared register (spreadsheet or tool already in the stack, WS25) with one row per public claim. Columns: claim ID, exact wording (Swedish), where it appears (URL/ad/email/social/print), claim type (A, B, C below), evidence file link, evidence date, who verified, legal sign-off date, re-verification date, status (live/paused/retired).

### 4.2 Claim classes

| Class | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A. Generic environmental or "sustainable" claims | "Miljövänlig resa", "klimatsmart", "grön safari", "hållbar", "ansvarsfull resa" as a promise | **Banned.** Replace with a specific claim (class B/C). If the word "ansvarsfullt resande" is used as the name of a policy page, the specification (what exactly we do) must be on the same page, in the first screen. |
| B. Specific, measurable, evidenced | "Alla guider på våra gruppresor är anställda och betalas enligt [standard]." / "Vi har kontrollerat att [X] av våra [Y] största boenden har en miljö- eller hållbarhetscertifiering." | Allowed after evidence file + legal sign-off + date + source on the same medium; re-verify at least every 12 months, or on supplier change. |
| C. Third-party certification or label | Travelife status; Fair Trade Tourism-certified lodges | Allowed only with a valid certificate, the scheme's logo-use terms read, and the scope stated exactly (company vs. a given lodge). Scope creep ("our trips are Fair Trade") is not allowed when only some suppliers are certified. |
| D. Forward-looking commitments / targets | "Vi ska ha X till 2028" | Allowed only with a published plan and measurable milestones; reporting annually (Directive recital/provisions on future environmental performance claims [VERIFY with counsel]). |
| E. Social impact claims | "Pengarna går till lokalsamhället" | Treated like class B. Needs the share, the recipient, the amount basis and the payment trail. |

### 4.3 Approval workflow (SLA two working days)
1. Author drafts claim in the register (Marketing, Product, Social).
2. Product confirms the operational fact with the supplier in writing (email counts).
3. Marketing checks wording against the rules (class, specificity, same-medium qualification).
4. Legal reviewer approves (batch review twice a week).
5. Publish; register status "live"; reviewer re-check date set.
Emergency pause: any employee can request that a claim be paused; Head of Marketing decides within four working hours. Paid ads with environmental wording go through the same workflow (WS06, WS07).

### 4.4 Standards for the checkers
- Konsumentverket: environmental claims in marketing must be factual, truthful and not misleading and are judged on the average consumer's overall impression; the trader must be able to prove the impression, not just the intent (https://www.konsumentverket.se/marknadsratt-foretag/miljopastaenden-regler-for-foretag/).
- The authority uses the ICC rules on environmental claims in assessing good marketing practice (same source).
- EU Commission guidance on Directive 2005/29/EC (unfair commercial practices) has a section on environmental claims (same source).
- Directive 2024/825 (applies 27 Sept 2026) as above.
- Package Travel Act and price information duties (R09) apply separately: "from" prices must be real bookable prices (R09 section on price information), including community-experience add-ons.
- A separate EU Green Claims Directive proposal had not been adopted as of the September 2026 source (https://www.hohenstein.us/en-us/services/regulatory-assessments/eu-green-deal/empco-compliance); monitor, do not plan around it.

### 4.5 Retrospective audit (first 30 days)
Scan sydafrikaresor.se, current ads, email templates, social posts, brochures and supplier-supplied copy for any environmental/social wording. Anything generic: remove or rewrite within 5 working days of discovery.

## 5. Product and supplier approach

### 5.1 Supplier sustainability screening (lightweight)
- A one-page supplier questionnaire in English (Appendix B) sent to DMCs, lodges and guide partners in the allocation and contracting rounds that start the week of 2 Nov 2026 (strategy section 13.2). Questions cover: certifications held (and certificate number), staff employment and local hiring, community ownership/benefit share, water/energy practice (self-reported), wildlife-contact policy, child-protection policy, health and safety, and willingness to be audited or photographed.
- **Self-reported answers are not claims.** They become class B claims only after verification by documents or a visit/fam trip.
- Scoring (internal, never published as a score): Green/Amber/Red per area. Red on child-protection or captive-animal contact = do not sell, Gate 0 / supply-readiness exclusion.
- Preference rule: when two suppliers are equal on price, quality and capacity, choose the one with credible third-party certification [ASSUMPTION SU7: internal policy proposal for CEO decision]. No sustainability premium that pushes a product past the margin guardrails (strategy section 14).

### 5.2 Third-party schemes to recognise (what exists, how to use)
- **Fair Trade Tourism (FTT):** an independent certification programme for tourism businesses in southern Africa (https://www.threetreehill.co.za/pages/fair-trade-tourism-2/); provides certification, business development, networking and marketing services (https://destinet.eu/resources/certificates/fair-trade-tourism). A secondary social-media source describes 180+ criteria for lodges, guesthouses and tour operators; unverified, check with FTT before quoting any number. Use: mark FTT-certified suppliers in the supplier database and, once verified with FTT, tag itineraries that include them. Open question: whether FTT has a Swedish-market licence/partner programme [VERIFY by contacting FTT in week 2].
- **GSTC:** the GSTC Tour Operator Standard is public domain for non-commercial use and the benchmark for tour operators (https://www.gstc.org/for-tour-operators/). GSTC itself does not certify companies; certification is by accredited bodies. Travelife for Tour Operators and Travel Agents has achieved GSTC status (sources: https://www.gstc.org/for-tour-operators/ ; https://www.3blmedia.com/news/travelife-achieves-recognized-global-sustainable-tourism-council-status-its-tour-operator-and ; check the current status wording on gstc.org before use, one source text says "recognized", another says "accredited"). Never write "GSTC-certified" about the company.
- Other schemes (national parks' concession conditions, B-BBEE ownership status for South African partners, Green Tourism, Green Key): [VERIFY] relevance by supplier; B-BBEE ownership data may be a useful objective indicator of local ownership but is a South African legal framework and should be described accurately [ASSUMPTION SU8: check with DMC].

### 5.3 Community-based product development (feeds WS02, L9)
Selection criteria for a "community-based experience" to be shown as such:
1. Owned, operated or co-governed by local community members (verify ownership documents or partner declaration).
2. Revenue flow is traceable: share paid to local operators documented in the DMC cost sheet.
3. Visitor management agreed with community (group size, photography, behaviour); no "poverty tourism" framing.
4. Safe, insured, licensed guides.
5. Sales-ready: capacity and price confirmed; fits the strategy supply-readiness gate (section 13.7: at least 60% of planned volume contracted, age policy checked, guide and DMC confirmed, margin at least 18% or 15% after partner commission, launch cap equal to contracted supply).
6. Customer fit: suits the 45+ leisure segments that dominate the plan (strategy section 7; verify with WS02).

Launch pipeline: long list 15 candidates by 15 Jan 2027, due-diligence on 6, fam visit or video call on 4, 2 launch candidates by 31 Mar 2027, 6 live by 31 Dec 2027, 10 by 31 Dec 2028 (O5). Experiences are offered as optional add-ons or inclusions in existing itineraries first (low supply risk, supports L6 revenue per traveller) before building dedicated departures.

### 5.4 Guided departures and guide welfare
The strategy plans about 41 guided departures needing 5-6 guides plus 2 reserves (unverified). Guide fair-treatment (employment status, pay, rest, training) is both a service-quality issue and a claim-substance issue. Action: document guide terms; only after documentation can the company say anything about guide treatment (class B). Link to WS23.

### 5.5 Climate approach (measure, inform, reduce, do not claim)
1. Choose a transparent calculation method for flights + ground transport + accommodation proxies (examples to evaluate: ICAO carbon calculator, other published methods) [VERIFY choice with analyst; no emissions figure is quoted in this plan].
2. Calculate an estimate per itinerary family (e.g. 10-14 day Cape Town + Garden Route + safari) and state the method in a footnote.
3. Present as information ("Beräknade utsläpp för flyg: ca X kg CO2e per person, metod: Y") only after the figure is verified and the same-medium specification is available.
4. Reduction levers within our control: itinerary design (fewer domestic flights, longer stays, consolidation), vehicle choice, supplier energy practice, paperless documents, office policy. Quantify before claiming.
5. Offsets: not marketed as neutralising. If the CEO wants an optional contribution scheme, it must be an opt-in, separate, transparent line (project, standard, price, share to project) and not a claim about the trip [ASSUMPTION SU9: decision for CEO, default is no].

## Appendix B. Supplier questionnaire (English, one page, to DMCs and lodges)

1. Certifications and standards held (name, certificate number, valid-to date, scope: whole business or one property). Attach copy.
2. Ownership and management: share owned by local community members or historically disadvantaged individuals (self-reported; documents on request).
3. Employment: number of employees; share local; written contracts; wages relative to sector minimum; training offered; guide employment status (employed/freelance) and typical rest days.
4. Community: named community partners or projects; how money flows (share, amount basis, payment trail); consent and visit rules.
5. Wildlife: any captive-animal contact, feeding or riding (yes/no, details); conservation or anti-poaching partners.
6. Child protection: policy in place (yes/no); no child-contact visits for tourists (confirm).

8. Health, safety and insurance: licences, guide qualifications, vehicle maintenance.
